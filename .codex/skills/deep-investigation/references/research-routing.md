# Research routing

## Entity route

Use when the final question is principally about a named company, institution, person, product, or other bounded actor.

Investigate:

- identity, ownership and boundaries;
- business or activity model;
- customers, suppliers and counterparties;
- finances or measurable results;
- technology or operational capability;
- governance, incentives and related parties;
- regulatory and legal record;
- external verification and peer comparison;
- controversies, alternative explanations and disconfirming evidence.

Do not mechanically create one chapter per dimension. Use them to find the central tension.

## Topic route

Use when the final question concerns an industry, policy, event, trend, market, controversy, or social phenomenon that crosses actors.

Investigate:

- working definition and exclusions;
- chronology and turning points;
- scale, distribution and affected populations;
- actors, incentives and power relationships;
- causal mechanisms;
- policy and legal environment;
- competing explanations;
- international or historical comparisons where useful;
- disconfirming evidence and uncertainty.

## Ambiguous requests

Choose the route matching the question's final subject:

- “Why is Company X losing share in the EV market?” → entity.
- “Why are EV price wars intensifying, including Company X?” → topic.

Record secondary route dimensions in the brief; do not create a third “hybrid” type.
