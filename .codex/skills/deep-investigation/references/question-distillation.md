# Question distillation before drafting

Use this gate after the first meaningful source collection and before fixing the outline. The “panel” is a set of analytical lenses, not independent witnesses or a substitute for external verification.

## Three required lenses

### Reader lens

Identify what a reasonably informed non-specialist would need explained:

- What is the most surprising verified fact or contrast?
- What does the subject do in plain language?
- Why does the issue matter outside investors or industry insiders?
- What remains confusing after reading the collected material?

### Market or system lens

Identify the assumptions behind the prevailing valuation, policy view, or institutional consensus:

- What is the documented consensus, and whose consensus is it?
- Which operating or causal drivers would have to persist for that view to hold?
- Which apparent tailwinds may be temporary, base-effect driven, or already priced in?
- Which risks or positive evidence may be underweighted?

For a non-company topic, replace “market” with the relevant system lens: policy implementers, affected institutions, labor market, public services, scientific field, or another actor that allocates resources.

### Controversy and disconfirmation lens

Look for tensions rather than allegations:

- Where do public statements and measurable outcomes diverge?
- What evidence would disprove the strongest emerging explanation?
- Which affected party, regulator, competitor, supplier, customer, or critic sees the issue differently?
- Which important claim rests only on inference, social media, a single source, or unavailable data?

Do not assert that a company “does not want” an issue discussed unless there is evidence of suppression, evasion, litigation, or refusal to answer. Do not infer motives from sales, holdings, price movements, or timing alone.

## Evidence discipline

Every observation must be labeled:

- `verified`: directly supported by one or more evidence IDs;
- `inference`: a reasoned interpretation that names its supporting evidence and an alternative explanation;
- `lead`: plausible but not yet verified; it becomes a research task, not article copy;
- `unknown`: important but currently unanswerable.

Panel notes may not introduce unsourced numbers, quotes, motives, market consensus, demographic claims, or current prices. A striking number without an evidence ID is a lead.

## Integrated question list

Distill three to five core questions. Each question records:

1. reader value — what a non-specialist learns that the headline does not explain;
2. tension — the conflicting facts, claims, incentives, or outcomes;
3. evidence base — existing evidence IDs;
4. evidence gap — the next documents, data, comparison, or interview needed;
5. disconfirmation — what finding would weaken or overturn the premise;
6. answerability — `ready`, `needs-research`, or `currently-unanswerable`.

A question need not already be answerable to be important, but it cannot become a chapter claim until evidence is sufficient. Avoid arbitrary deadlines such as “can the company replace an IP within 30 days?” unless that interval has operational or contractual meaning.

## Drafting gate

Do not lock the outline while all core questions are merely leads, while the three lenses repeat the same assumption, or while no disconfirming evidence has been identified. The panel is for generating and challenging questions, not confirming a preferred thesis or selecting the easiest angle.

Do not impose fixed word, chapter, source-count, or chart quotas. Adequacy depends on whether the core questions, mechanisms, counterevidence, and material uncertainties are covered.

### Positive example

`[verified, E1] THE MONSTERS rose from 23.3% to 38.1% of revenue while five other IPs also exceeded RMB2 billion. Core question: can the broader IP portfolio offset a slowdown in the largest IP? Gap: IP-level quarterly sales and repeat purchase. Disconfirmation: several non-THE MONSTERS IPs sustain growth after the leading IP decelerates.`

### Negative example

`The founder cashed out because he does not believe the valuation, and children are the core buyers.`

This assigns motives and customer composition without evidence. Record the disclosed sale as a verified fact, and separately research stated use of proceeds, voting control, customer age data, and alternative explanations.
